Where every film was shot

Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, knows where {{source.n_films|int}} films were shot: {{source.n_locs|int}} filming locations, from Pinewood Studios to Lake Como. This map puts them all in one place, shows which countries play host to other countries' films, and follows James Bond around the world.

Every film is made somewhere, and quite often it is made somewhere other than where it is set, or where it comes from. Wikidata records a filming location for tens of thousands of films, each with coordinates. Put them on a map and you get a picture of the world's film industry as the open record sees it: a few huge production cities, a ring of studios and suburbs around them, and a scattering of deserts, islands and old towns that turn up in film after film.

That record is far from complete, and its gaps are not random. {{source.us_pct|pc}} of the films here are American, and films with more Wikipedia articles have more locations recorded. Many locations are whole countries or states rather than places: {{source.plev_pct.country|pc}} of film-location records just say a country, and {{source.plev_pct.region|pc}} a state or region. Those are kept off the point map, so a country's middle never looks like a film set; they still count when I ask which countries host which films. Use the map to explore, and read every number as "what Wikidata knows".

    03 · Home and awayWho films in whose country

    A film is shot abroad when a location is outside every country Wikidata gives as its country of origin, so co-productions are at home in every partner country. By that measure {{abroad.pct|pc}} of films with an origin and a located filming place were shot at least partly abroad ({{abroad.pct_ex_idn|pc}} leaving out Indonesia's films, which almost all list only Indonesia).

    Canada hosts the most foreign productions by far: {{hosts.can.foreign|int}} films, {{hosts.can_from_us_pct|pc}} of them American. The United States ({{hosts.usa.foreign|int}}), the United Kingdom ({{hosts.gbr.foreign|int}}), France ({{hosts.top_foreign.3.foreign|int}}) and Spain ({{hosts.esp.foreign|int}}) follow, then Mexico ({{hosts.mex.foreign|int}}). In smaller places foreign films dominate: {{hosts.mlt.foreign_pct|pc}} of the films shot in Malta come from somewhere else, {{hosts.mar.foreign_pct|pc}} in Morocco, {{hosts.bgr.foreign_pct|pc}} in Bulgaria, {{hosts.isl.foreign_pct|pc}} in Iceland and {{hosts.nzl.foreign_pct|pc}} in New Zealand.

Going the other way, {{leave.gbr.pct|pc}} of British films and {{leave.ind.pct|pc}} of Indian films with a located place were shot partly abroad, against {{leave.usa.pct|pc}} of American and {{leave.fra.pct|pc}} of French films.

    By decade

    The share of films shot abroad has risen, but less than you might expect: from {{decades.4.pct_ex_idn|pc}} of films in the 1950s to {{decades.9.pct_ex_idn|pc}} in the 2000s and {{decades.10.pct_ex_idn|pc}} in the 2010s (leaving out Indonesia). American films were already travelling in the 1950s and 1960s ({{decades.5.us_pct|pc}} in the 1960s) when Hollywood went to Spain, Italy and Britain for cheaper crews and for earnings that could not be taken out of Europe. What changed more is where they went.

    04 · Stand-insPlaces that play somewhere else

    Some cities are filmed mostly by other people. In Valletta and the rest of Malta, {{hubs.pick.Valletta.foreign_pct|pc}} of films are foreign, mainly British and American. Budapest ({{hubs.pick.Budapest.foreign_pct|pc}}), Vancouver ({{hubs.vancouver.foreign_pct|pc}}) and Toronto ({{hubs.pick.Toronto.foreign_pct|pc}}) are the big examples of cities that host American productions. These are the classic stand-ins: Toronto for New York, Budapest for half of Europe, Malta's water tanks for the open sea.

    A caution: Wikidata does not say what a location stood in for. A separate property records where a film is set, but it is not in this data, so I cannot show that Toronto played New York in a given film, only that most of the films shot there come from elsewhere. And countries such as Morocco, New Zealand and Iceland, which host many foreign films, are mostly recorded only as the whole country: {{hosts.nzl.n|int}} films were shot in New Zealand, but only {{hubs.pick.Wellington.n|int}} have a location in Wellington and {{hubs.pick.Auckland.n|int}} in Auckland.

    05 · James BondThe 25 Eon films

    The Bond films are the obvious test of a map like this. Wikidata gives {{bond.n_pairs|int}} filming locations for the 25 official Eon Productions films, from Dr. No ({{bond.films.0.y|raw}}) to No Time to Die ({{bond.films.24.y|raw}}): {{bond.n_locs|int}} distinct places in {{bond.n_countries|int}} countries and territories. Pinewood Studios is listed for {{bond.pinewood|int}} of the 25. Beyond the UK, the United States appears for {{bond.countries.1.2|int}} films, then Switzerland, Italy and the Bahamas ({{bond.countries.2.2|int}} each) and Spain ({{bond.countries.5.2|int}}).

    06 · The gapsWhat the record leaves out

    This is a map of documented filming, not of filming. The films come from {{origins.n_countries|int}} countries, but the United States alone accounts for {{source.us_pct|pc}} of them, and Indonesia for {{origins.top.1.pct|pc}}, almost all entered with the single location "Indonesia". India, which makes more films each year than any other country, has {{origins.ind|int}} here; China {{origins.chn|int}} and Nigeria {{origins.nga|int}}. Wikidata's film coverage follows Wikipedia's, and Wikipedia's follows the languages and fan bases of its editors.

    07 · How I did thisMethods and caveats

    Films and places. Every Wikidata item that is an instance of film (Q11424) with a filming location (P915) that has coordinates, from one QLever query: {{source.n_rows|int}} rows, because a film repeats for every combination of release date, country of origin and location country. I kept one record per film and per location: {{source.n_films|int}} films, {{source.n_locs|int}} locations and {{source.n_pairs|int}} film-location pairs. A film's year is its earliest publication date ({{source.n_dated|int}} have one). {{source.n_notitle|int}} films have no English title in Wikidata and are shown by their Wikidata ID.

    Countries, states and sites. Each location is a country (its Wikidata ID is a country's, a UK nation's, a former state's or a continent's; {{source.lev.country|int}} locations), a region (a state, province or region in Natural Earth, or a large named area; {{source.lev.region|int}}), a city or town (its name matches a Natural Earth populated place within 40 km; {{source.lev.city|int}}), or a site (anything else, from studios to streets and villages; {{source.lev.site|int}}). Country and region points are centroids and are never mapped as places. Small territories such as Hong Kong, Malta, Monaco and Singapore count as cities. Its country is the present-day country its point falls in.

    Home and abroad. A film's countries of origin (P495) are its home; a film from the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia or Czechoslovakia is at home in any successor state, and crown dependencies and overseas territories count as home for the UK, the United States, France, the Netherlands and Denmark. {{source.n_no_origin|int}} films have no country of origin and are left out of these figures.

    Cities. For the city rankings each city, town or site is grouped with the nearest Natural Earth place of 250,000 people or more within 32 km. Bond. The 25 Eon films are a hand list of Wikidata IDs (in the code), each checked against title and year; the 1967 Casino Royale, not an Eon film, is left out.

    What this is not. It is not a complete list of where films were shot, and it cannot say where a film is set, what a location stood in for, or how long a crew spent there. Popularity is Wikimedia sitelinks, not box office.
