Wikidata knows where {{source.n_films|int}} films were shot. I put every one of those filming locations on a map to find the world's film cities, the countries that play host to other countries' films, and every place James Bond has been, and to see how much of the picture is simply missing.

Films are rarely made where they say they are. A Western might be shot in Spain, a New York thriller in Toronto, a desert epic in Morocco. Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, records a filming location with map coordinates for {{source.n_films|int}} films, at {{source.n_locs|int}} places: studios, streets, beaches, castles, whole cities and sometimes whole countries.

That last part matters. {{source.plev_pct.country|pc}} of the film-location records just say a country, and another {{source.plev_pct.region|pc}} a state or region. Put those on a map and you get fake hotspots in the middle of Indonesia or California. So I sorted every location into country, region, city or town, and site, and only mapped the last two. The countries still count when I ask who films where.

The film cities

I grouped each location with the nearest big city within 32 km, so Pinewood Studios counts for London and Santa Monica for Los Angeles. Four places stand out: Los Angeles with {{hubs.la.n|int}} films, New York with {{hubs.ny.n|int}}, London with {{hubs.london.n|int}} and Paris with {{hubs.paris.n|int}}. Vancouver is a distant fifth with {{hubs.vancouver.n|int}}. Pinewood alone is listed for {{sites.pinewood|int}} films, more than any other single site.

{{IMG|b_cities.png|Horizontal bar chart of the twenty cities with most films: Los Angeles 1,514, New York City 1,350, London 1,118, Paris 933, then Vancouver, Montreal, Hong Kong, Toronto, Rome and Madrid with roughly 290 to 450 each, and gold numbers on the right giving each city's rank when films are weighted by popularity|The busiest filming cities in Wikidata, with each city's rank when films are weighted by popularity.}}

Weighting each film by its number of Wikipedia articles and other Wikimedia pages (sitelinks, a rough measure of fame) reshuffles the list lower down. Long Beach jumps from {{hubs.pick.Long Beach.rank_n|ord}} to {{hubs.pick.Long Beach.rank_w|ord}}, and Ooty, a hill town in Tamil Nadu with {{hubs.pick.Ooty.n|int}} mostly Indian films, falls to {{hubs.pick.Ooty.rank_w|ord}}. That says less about the films than about whose films Wikipedia writes about.

Who films in whose country

Wikidata also records each film's countries of origin. If a location is outside all of them, the film was shot abroad there. By that measure {{abroad.pct|pc}} of films were shot at least partly abroad. Canada hosts by far the most foreign productions, {{hosts.can.foreign|int}}, and {{hosts.can_from_us_pct|pc}} of them are American. The United States, the United Kingdom, France and Spain follow. In smaller places, foreign films are most of what gets shot: {{hosts.mlt.foreign_pct|pc}} in Malta, {{hosts.mar.foreign_pct|pc}} in Morocco, {{hosts.bgr.foreign_pct|pc}} in Bulgaria and {{hosts.isl.foreign_pct|pc}} in Iceland.

The share of films shot abroad has crept up, from {{decades.4.pct_ex_idn|pc}} in the 1950s to {{decades.9.pct_ex_idn|pc}} in the 2000s (leaving out Indonesian films, which almost all list only "Indonesia"). The bigger change is where films go. Spain's foreign productions peak in the {{host_peak.ESP|raw}}s, the era of European co-productions and westerns shot around Almería. Canada's peak in the {{host_peak.CAN|raw}}s, Bulgaria's too, and Hungary's and Iceland's in the {{host_peak.HUN|raw}}s.

Stand-ins

Some cities are filmed mostly by outsiders. In Valletta and the rest of Malta {{hubs.pick.Valletta.foreign_pct|pc}} of the films are foreign; in Budapest {{hubs.pick.Budapest.foreign_pct|pc}}, Vancouver {{hubs.vancouver.foreign_pct|pc}} and Toronto {{hubs.pick.Toronto.foreign_pct|pc}}, mostly American. These are the famous stand-ins, cities that play somewhere else. I have to be careful here, though: Wikidata's filming locations do not say what a place stood in for, so the data show who comes to film, not what they pretended it was.

Following Bond

Bond was the first thing I wanted to look up. The 25 official Eon films, from Dr. No in {{bond.films.0.y|raw}} to No Time to Die in {{bond.films.24.y|raw}}, have {{bond.n_pairs|int}} recorded filming locations, {{bond.n_locs|int}} distinct places in {{bond.n_countries|int}} countries and territories. Pinewood is listed for {{bond.pinewood|int}} of them. After the UK come the United States ({{bond.countries.1.2|int}} films) and then Switzerland, Italy and the Bahamas ({{bond.countries.2.2|int}} each).

{{IMG|b_bond.png|World map with countries shaded gold by how many Bond films list a location there, darkest for the United Kingdom and United States, and dots for filming places coloured from teal for the 1960s to dark red for the 2020s, clustered in Britain, the Alps, Italy, Spain and the Caribbean, with outliers in Japan, China, Thailand, Brazil, Chile and Azerbaijan|Filming locations of the 25 Eon James Bond films in Wikidata.}}

Bond is also the best illustration of the data's biggest weakness. Spectre has {{bond.most.n|int}} locations in Wikidata; Licence to Kill has {{bond.fewest.n|int}}. The early films did not travel less (Goldfinger went to Switzerland, Kentucky and Miami), they are just written down in less detail.

What the map leaves out

This is a map of documented filming. {{source.us_pct|pc}} of the films are American. India, which makes more films each year than any other country, has {{origins.ind|int}}; Nigeria has {{origins.nga|int}}. Popular films are described in far more detail: films with fewer than five sitelinks average {{pop.tiers.0.locs|f1}} recorded locations, films with 50 or more average {{pop.tiers.3.locs|f1}}, and they are four times as likely to be shot abroad. Some of that is real; some is just attention.

How I did this

One query to Wikidata, through QLever, returned every film with a filming location that has coordinates, with its release dates, countries of origin and sitelinks, and each location's country. The {{source.n_rows|int}} rows boil down to {{source.n_films|int}} films and {{source.n_locs|int}} locations. Each location is classed as a country, a region, a city or town (matched to Natural Earth's populated places) or a site; only cities, towns and sites go on the map, and each is grouped with the nearest big city for the rankings. A film counts as shot abroad wherever a location falls outside all its countries of origin, with co-productions at home in every partner country. The Bond films are a hand list of the 25 Eon films. The paper sets out every step and caveat.
